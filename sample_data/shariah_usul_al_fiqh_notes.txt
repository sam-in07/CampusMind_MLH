Principles of Islamic Jurisprudence (QSIS 1101) — Principles of Usul al-Fiqh

1. Foundational Sources:
- The Holy Qur'an: The divine, recited revelation (Wahy Matluw) sent down to Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Definite in authentication (Qat'i al-Thubut).
- The Prophetic Sunnah: Actions, statements, and tacit consents of the Messenger. Explains and contextualizes the Qur'anic principles.

2. Agreed Secondary Sources:
- Ijma' (Consensus): The unanimous consensus of the Mujtahid scholars of the Muslim community in an era following the Prophet's death on a legal ruling.
- Qiyas (Analogical Deduction): Extension of a legal ruling from an established original case (Asl) to a new case (Far') based on the effective cause ('Illah) common to both.

3. Four Pillars of Qiyas:
- Asl: The original case mentioned in primary texts.
- Far': The new case requiring a legal ruling.
- Hukm: The ruling established for the Asl.
- 'Illah: The underlying ratio decidendi or legal cause.
